A Playbook by David Gabeau

5 Steps to Building Anthropic's NYC Startup Ecosystem

Hi, my name is David Gabeau and I'm applying to be Anthropic's Claude Evangelist for NYC because I believe the proliferation of safe and beneficial AI is the defining work of our time. For the past four years I've been building Tapestry, an open social protocol, the developer ecosystem around it, and a 12-person team shipping AI products to 160,000 people.

I build with Claude every day and it would be a privilege to show founders building right now why they should do the same. This website is a non-exhaustive demo of how I would think about building Anthropic's NYC startup function from zero.

01

Step 01 — Ecosystem Intelligence

You can't activate what you can't see.

To do this job well you need to understand the NYC startup ecosystem deeply. Who is building what. Which funds are most active. Which accelerators have the best AI cohorts. Where Claude is already showing up and where it isn't yet.

I have been thinking about this in my spare time. I built AI Atlas NYC as a side project because I genuinely find the NYC AI ecosystem interesting. Two years as an analyst at USV gave me the habit of reading markets this way. The venture and accelerator relationships I have built since keep it current.

Ask it anything below. AI Atlas NYC was built with Claude Code, updates agentically, and this demo pulls live data through an API I built from the ground up.

Step 02 — Network Activation

The fastest path to 40 activations is one right conversation.

The NYC AI ecosystem runs through a small number of funds and accelerators. USV. a16z. Lerer Hippeau. Bessemer. BoxGroup. ERA. Techstars NYC. Thrive. And others. If the partners at those firms think of you first when a portfolio company asks what AI to build on, you have distribution that no content budget can buy.

I would activate both my existing venture network and build new relationships through 1:1 calls and small group dinners, with every warm intro mapped to a clear GTM handoff. Alongside that, a selective NYC Claude Slack — high signal, low noise — where founders get early access to events, credits, and each other.

03

Step 03 — IRL Activation

Events that end with repos, not slide decks.

Every event has one goal: developers who show up curious leave with a working Claude integration.

The Claude Summer Series is four category-specific demo days at top NYC fund offices. Each one anchors Claude to a vertical where it wins. Consumer at a16z. Fintech at Bessemer. Dev tools at Hugging Face. Health at BoxGroup.

In the fall: build-athons at Columbia, NYU, and CUNY. Live-streamed demo days. Target: 80% of teams shipping a working Claude integration by Sunday night.

Concepts only. Events subject to internal planning and approval.

04

Step 04 — Technical Content

Written by someone who has shipped this. Not someone who read the docs.

Two blog series to start.

Series one: tool use and function calling. The most important concept for any founder building an agentic product. Two posts, two audiences. Beginner and production level. Both with working repos. Distributed to 15,000 existing blog subscribers on day one.

Series two: NYC Founders Building on Claude. Short, specific, technical spotlights. What they built, which models they use, what they learned. Pitched to Anthropic marketing for amplification through Claude's social channels.

Step 05 — Measure Everything

Every touchpoint feeds the signal back.

The metric is net new startups activated on the Claude API. Everything else is a leading indicator.

But the loop only closes if ecosystem signal goes back to the right people. What are NYC founders building? Where is Claude winning? Where is it losing and why? What features are founders asking for that do not exist yet?

A monthly signal report to product and GTM teams. Not a vanity deck. Actual developer sentiment, actual gaps, actual requests. The evangelist role is not just outbound. It is the feedback mechanism that makes the product better for the people it is trying to serve.
