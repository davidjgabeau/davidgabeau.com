Step 01 · Ecosystem Intelligence

I would start by knowing the ecosystem cold.

To do this job well you need to understand the NYC startup ecosystem deeply. Who is building what. Which funds are most active. Which accelerators have the best AI cohorts. Where Claude is already showing up and where it isn't yet.

I have been thinking about this in my spare time. I built AI Atlas NYC as a side project because I genuinely find the NYC AI ecosystem interesting. Two years as an analyst at USV gave me the habit of reading markets this way. The venture and accelerator relationships I have built since keep it current.

Ask it anything below. AI Atlas NYC was built with Claude Code, updates agentically, and this demo pulls live data through an API I built from the ground up.

Step 02 · Network Activation

I would build relationships with the funds, accelerators, and communities that matter.

The NYC AI ecosystem runs through a small number of funds and accelerators. USV. a16z. Lerer Hippeau. Bessemer. BoxGroup. ERA. Techstars NYC. Thrive. And others. If the partners at those firms think of you first when a portfolio company asks what AI to build on, you have distribution that no content budget can buy.

I would activate both my existing venture network and build new relationships through 1:1 calls and small group dinners, with every warm intro mapped to a clear GTM handoff. Alongside that, a selective NYC Claude Slack, high signal, low noise, where founders get early access to events, credits, and each other.

Step 03 · IRL Activation

I would show up in person and make founders famous.

The best way to build credibility in a startup community is to celebrate it. Demo days that showcase the founders already building on Claude create a room where the next wave of founders want to be in.

The Claude Summer Series is four category-specific demo days hosted at top NYC fund offices. Consumer at a16z. Fintech at Bessemer. Dev tools at Hugging Face. Health at BoxGroup. Each event anchors Claude to a vertical, surfaces the best builders, and puts them in front of the investors and peers who matter.

Separately, build-athons at Columbia, NYU, and CUNY bring in the next generation. Two-day format, live-streamed demo days. The goal is simple: everyone leaves with something they built on Claude.

For each event I would define the builder outcome before defining the format. A good event should answer: who is this for, what will they build, what Claude use case will they understand better, and what follow-up should happen within 48 hours.

Step 04 · Technical Content

I would create content that scales beyond any single event.

Technical content scales in a way that events cannot. One good post or walkthrough reaches founders who will never show up to a hackathon. I would write consistently, recruit others at Anthropic to contribute, and push what makes sense into video over time.

But the best way to show a founder what Claude can do is to let them experience it. Here is a demo I built with Claude Code. Founders can enter their startup details and get a plan for how to best use Claude.

The content should answer the founder's real question

Most founders do not need another abstract explanation of frontier models. They need to know whether Claude can help with a specific product or workflow this week.

  • Which Claude model should I use?
  • What is the fastest useful thing I can build?
  • How do I evaluate quality?
  • What will this cost if it works?
  • How do I move from prototype to production?

Step 05 · Measure Everything

I would measure everything and close the loop.

Net new startups activated on the Claude API is the primary metric. Everything else feeds into it. How many VC partnerships are generating warm intros. How many events converted attendees into active builders. How many founders in the Slack community are shipping on Claude. What the content is reaching and who is acting on it.

The evangelist role is also the feedback mechanism. What are NYC founders building? Where is Claude winning? Where is it losing and why? A monthly signal report back to product and GTM teams keeps the loop closed.

I would go to underserved tech communities.

There is one more thing worth saying.

Dario wrote in Machines of Loving Grace that AI could give every person on earth access to the kind of expert advisor that only wealthy people have today. I think about that a lot.

The NYC startup ecosystem is not just a16z and Bessemer. It is CUNY. It is a first-generation founder in the Bronx who has never been in a room with a VC. It is a high school student at Comp Sci High who is about to build something nobody has thought of yet. Getting Claude into those rooms is not charity. It is the mission.

I would show up there before anyone asked. I already do.

First 90 Days

How I would start

If I joined Anthropic in this role I would start by turning this playbook into a focused operating cadence. The first 90 days would be about learning the current motion, finding the highest-fit startup segments, running a few high-signal experiments, and building a repeatable path from ecosystem trust to active Claude usage.

Days 1 to 30: Learn the motion and map the opportunity

I would start by understanding Anthropic's existing startup motion: current goals, partner channels, activation data, sales handoff process, and product feedback loops. In parallel I would map the NYC ecosystem around Claude-relevant use cases and identify the accounts, partners, and communities most likely to produce active API usage.

  • Meet with Startups GTM, Developer Relations, Marketing, and Product
  • Audit current activation paths and follow-up loops
  • Identify high-fit NYC startups and founder communities
  • Build a short list of priority fund and accelerator partners
  • Run founder discovery conversations around Claude use cases and switching moments

Days 31 to 60: Run focused activation experiments

Once the map is clear I would run a small number of focused programs designed to create real technical activation. Three sharp experiments with strong follow-up over ten broad events with weak conversion.

  • Run the first partner-led Claude startup workshop
  • Launch one vertical starter kit based on observed founder demand
  • Host office hours for founders already evaluating Claude
  • Track API keys created, first builds completed, and repeat usage
  • Package learnings into a repeatable format

Days 61 to 90: Scale what works

By month three I would double down on the programs showing real usage signal. The goal is to move from promising activity to a repeatable operating system for NYC startup activation.

  • Scale the strongest partner or community channel
  • Turn the best-performing workshop into a repeatable format
  • Publish or expand the highest-performing starter kit
  • Build a lightweight reporting cadence around logos, active usage, and learnings
  • Create a feedback loop from founders back to Product, Docs, and GTM

By the end of 90 days the goal would be simple: a clearer map of NYC's highest-fit startup opportunities, a few repeatable ways to activate them, and measurable evidence that the ecosystem motion is turning into active Claude usage.
