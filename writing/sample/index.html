A Note on Writing Here

A template preview — every element a post might use, rendered in the same type and color as the rest of the site.

David Gabeau · June 2026 · 3 min read

For a long time my writing lived on someone else's domain. That was fine — it is where the readers were — but the words always looked like the platform, not like me. This page is the opposite bet: a quiet reading column, a serif that has opinions, and nothing on the screen that isn't the sentence you are reading.

The column is forty rems wide on purpose. It holds roughly seventy characters a line, which is about as far as the eye likes to travel before it wants to come home. Everything else — the dark mode, the gold accents, the way links draw themselves under a word — is borrowed straight from the homepage, so a post never feels like a different website.

What a post can hold

A good essay rarely needs more than prose, but when it does, the pieces should already be beautiful. Here is a link back home styled with a fine gold underline that thickens when you hover it. Here is some emphasis that carries weight and some emphasis that leans. And here is a list, because some ideas arrive in threes:

  • Type that is comfortable to read for a thousand words, not just a headline.
  • Structure — headings, breaks, quotes — that you can skim without losing the thread.
  • Restraint, so the reader forgets they are looking at a screen at all.

When a sentence deserves to stand alone, it can:

The best interface for an idea is the one that disappears.

And when someone else said it better, a quotation gives them the floor without stealing the room:

The details are not the details. They make the design.

Smaller things, handled

Inline code reads cleanly mid-sentence, and a full block keeps its own quiet box:

function greet(name) {
  return `Hello, ${name}.`;
}

Images sit in the column with a soft border and a caption underneath, so a screenshot or a photo never crashes into the prose.

After a break like that one, a new movement begins. The ornament is the same one that closes the homepage, so the whole site keeps speaking in one voice. When you are ready, hand me the document and this becomes your first real post.1

  1. Footnotes look like this — for the asides that would otherwise interrupt the line.
